1 Negative numbersNumbers below zero, and how to add and take away with them 2:15

Negative numbers are numbers below zero, like the temperature on a very cold day: −3°C.

On a number line, zero is in the middle. Negatives are on the left, positives are on the right. Further right means bigger, so −2 is bigger than −5.

  • Adding: move right.
  • Taking away: move left.
  • Adding a negative is the same as taking away: 3 + (−5) = 3 − 5
  • Taking away a negative is the same as adding: 1 − (−3) = 1 + 3

Two minus signs next to each other make a plus.

2 Multiplying and dividing negativesSame signs or different signs? 1:14

When you multiply or divide with negative numbers, do it in two steps:

  1. Work out the numbers, ignoring the signs.
  2. Then choose the sign.
  • Same signs → answer is positive. −3 × −4 = 12
  • Different signs → answer is negative. 3 × −4 = −12

Dividing uses exactly the same rule.

Same signs: positive. Different signs: negative.

3 HCF: Highest Common FactorPrime factor trees and a Venn diagram 1:56

The HCF is the biggest number that goes into both numbers.

  1. Split each number into primes with a factor tree. Start with the smallest prime, 2.
  2. Write each number as primes multiplied: 12 = 2 × 2 × 3, 18 = 2 × 3 × 3
  3. Put the primes in a Venn diagram. Primes they share go in the middle.
  4. Multiply the middle: 2 × 3 = 6. The HCF of 12 and 18 is 6.

Middle for HCF. Everything for LCM.

4 LCM: Lowest Common MultipleThe first number in both times tables 1:21

Multiples are the numbers in a times table.

To find the LCM, list the multiples of both numbers and find the smallest one that is in both lists.

4: 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24…
6: 6, 12, 18, 24…

24 is in both lists too, but 12 comes first. So the LCM of 4 and 6 is 12.

We need the LCM to add and subtract fractions with different bottoms.

LCM = the smallest number in both times tables.

5 What is a fraction?Equal parts, equivalent fractions and simplifying 2:00

A fraction is part of a whole. The parts must be equal.

  • The bottom number: how many equal parts in total.
  • The top number: how many parts we colour in.

Equivalent fractions are the same amount with different names: 1 over 212 = 2 over 424 = 4 over 848

To make one, multiply the top and the bottom by the same number. To simplify, divide the top and bottom by their HCF: 6 over 868 → 3 over 434

Whatever you do to the top, do to the bottom.

6 Adding fractionsMake the bottoms match, then add the tops 1:14

If the bottoms are the same, add the tops. The bottom stays the same: 1 over 515 + 2 over 525 = 3 over 535

Never add the bottoms. The size of the parts does not change.

If the bottoms are different, the parts are different sizes:

  1. Find the LCM of the bottoms (3 and 4 → 12).
  2. Change both fractions: 1 over 313 = 4 over 12412, 1 over 414 = 3 over 12312
  3. Add the tops: 4 over 12412 + 3 over 12312 = 7 over 12712

Make the bottoms match, then add the tops.

7 Subtracting fractionsMake the bottoms match, then take away the tops 1:10

Subtracting works just like adding.

Same bottoms: take away the tops. 4 over 545 − 1 over 515 = 3 over 535

Different bottoms:

  1. Find the LCM of the bottoms (4 and 3 → 12).
  2. Change both fractions: 3 over 434 = 9 over 12912, 1 over 313 = 4 over 12412
  3. Take away the tops: 9 over 12912 − 4 over 12412 = 5 over 12512

Make the bottoms match, then take away the tops.

8 Multiplying fractionsTops times tops, bottoms times bottoms 1:09

With fractions, times means "of". 2 over 323 × 3 over 434 means 2 over 323 of 3 over 434.

In the video, the answer is where the coloured parts overlap: 6 boxes out of 12.

The quick way:

  • Multiply the tops: 2 × 3 = 6
  • Multiply the bottoms: 3 × 4 = 12

2 over 323 × 3 over 434 = 6 over 12612

You do not need the bottoms to match when multiplying.

Tops times tops. Bottoms times bottoms.

9 Dividing fractionsKeep, Change, Flip 1:15

Dividing asks: how many fit in? How many quarters fit into 3 wholes? 12.

The quick method is Keep, Change, Flip:

  1. Keep the first fraction.
  2. Change ÷ into ×.
  3. Flip the second fraction upside down.

Then multiply: 1 over 212 ÷ 1 over 414 = 1 over 212 × 4 over 141 = 4 over 242 = 2

Keep, Change, Flip. Then multiply.

10 Percentage increase and decreaseFind 10% first 2:18

Per cent means "out of 100".

The key trick: find 10% first by dividing by 10. 10% of £80 = £8, so 20% = £16.

  • Increase: find the percentage, then add it on. £80 + 20% = £80 + £16 = £96
  • Decrease: find the percentage, then take it away. £50 − 30% = £50 − £15 = £35

With a calculator: increase by 20% → × 1.2. Decrease by 30% → × 0.7.

Find the percentage. Then add it or take it away.

11 Solving equationsKeep it balanced: do the same to both sides 2:36

An equation has a mystery number, x. The = sign means both sides are balanced.

Golden rule: do the same thing to both sides.

Undo each step with the inverse:

  • + is undone by −, and − by +
  • × is undone by ÷, and ÷ by ×

Two-step: 2x + 1 = 11
− 1 from both sides: 2x = 10
÷ 2 both sides: x = 5

Check: 2 × 5 + 1 = 11 ✓

Undo the + or − first, then the × or ÷. Always check.
